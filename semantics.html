Semantics

The study of how the form of a linguistic expression relates to its meaning: its sense and its reference.

Form and meaning

Every linguistic sign has two sides: a form (the sounds or letters) and a meaning. Semantics studies the connection between the two.

The principle of compositionality says that the meaning of the whole is the meaning of its parts plus the way they are combined. Dog bites man and man bites dog use the same words, but the combination changes everything.

Two sides of meaning

sense noun
The mental representation of a meaning: the concept in our heads.
reference noun
The actual entities in the world that an expression points to: the real thing.

Some expressions have a sense but no reference. We all know what a unicorn is, but there is nothing in the world it refers to.

And different senses can share one reference. The country that hosted the 2008 Olympics and the country whose capital is Beijing are two different ideas that point to the same place.

Our notes

What surprised us is that a word can have a meaning even when nothing in the world matches it. We all know what a unicorn or a dragon is, but there's nothing real to point at.

We also noticed something about the China example from class: "the most populous country" doesn't refer to China anymore, because India passed it in 2023. So the sense of an expression can stay the same while its reference changes over time.

Lexical semantics

The sense and reference of individual words.

Three ways to define a word's sense

Dictionary-style definitions
Describing a word with other words, the way a dictionary does.
Mental image definitions
The picture that comes to mind when we hear the word.
Usage-based definitions
What a word means based on how people actually use it.

What words refer to

Proper names
Refer to one specific entity. Scarlet Witch is a Marvel character.
Common nouns
Refer to a set of entities in the world. Does your sister have a cat?
Adjectives
Refer to a set of entities: everything that is green.
Intransitive verbs
Refer to the set of entities that do the action. Felipe runs.

Meaning relations

Relationships between words are defined in terms of their reference.

hyponymy
X is a hyponym of Y when the set X refers to is always included in the set Y refers to. Poodle is a hyponym (subset) of dog, and dog is a hypernym (superset) of poodle.
synonymy
Words with the same reference. Sister / female sibling.
antonymy
Words that express opposites. Complementary antonyms have no middle ground (alive / dead). Gradable antonyms sit on a scale (hot / warm / cold).

Our notes

Our hyponym chain: gallo pinto → breakfast dish → food. Every gallo pinto is food, but not all food is gallo pinto, so it works exactly like poodle and dog.

Gradable antonyms were easy to see (hot, warm, cold). Complementary ones were trickier. Alive and dead is clear, but we argued about open and closed, because a door can be half open. The categories aren't always as clean as the definitions.

Compositional semantics

How the meanings of words combine to form the meanings of phrases and sentences.

It is raining today.

Compositional. Add up the meaning of each word and you get the meaning of the sentence.

It is raining cats and dogs. No animals involved.

Non-compositional. The meaning of the whole is not the sum of its parts. It is figurative, not literal.

Semantic ambiguity

homonyms
Same pronunciation and spelling, different meaning. bat (animal) / bat (baseball)
homophones
Same pronunciation, different spelling and meaning. see / sea, here / hear
homographs
Same spelling, different meaning; the pronunciation may be the same or different. ring /rɪŋ/ and ring /rɪŋ/ are also homonyms.
heteronyms
Homographs with different pronunciations. bass /beɪs/ (music) and bass /bæs/ (fish)

Our notes

In Spanish we say estar en las nubes when someone is distracted, and obviously nobody is in the clouds. If you translate it word by word for an English speaker it makes no sense, so you have to explain the whole meaning.

Another one we use all the time is ponerse las pilas. Nobody puts batteries on; it just means to start working harder. The closest thing in English would be "get your act together."

Propositions and truth

The sense of a sentence is a proposition: something that can be true or false in the world. Only sentences express propositions; words and phrases don't.

Entailment

One proposition entails another when, if the first is true, the second must be true. All dogs bark entails my dog barks. But Mexico is in Latin America and Danny speaks French are simply unrelated.
